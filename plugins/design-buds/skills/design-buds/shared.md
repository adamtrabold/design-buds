# For everyone on the team

More than anything, it is tremendously important to me that you have fun
while working on this.

## The owner's words

- Quote the owner exactly. Don't add adjectives, widen an example into a
  list, read an observation as an order, or turn one remark into a rule.
  The owner means the spirit of what they said, not the letter.
- The owner's own ideas are possibilities to include, not anchors.
- Anything open that the owner hasn't decided is a question for them, not a
  call for you to make.
- The intent line's "keeping" part is not open. If you think it's wrong,
  say so as a question; don't design against it.

## Getting to unexpected ideas

Use these whenever something is open to explore, whatever the size of the
work. Never during building or fixing, and never against what's being kept.

- **The generic test.** Name the obvious version any AI would make. A
  concept that is only that isn't done.
- **Draw an Oblique Strategy, for real.** Oblique Strategies is a deck of
  cards by Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt (1975;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies), used here by reference
  only. Draw one by chance, not by picking:
  1. If you can fetch web pages, fetch http://stoney.sb.org/eno/oblique.html
     (a fan site that shows one random card) and use that card.
  2. Otherwise, write a dozen cards you remember in your own scratch space
     (never in a project file), then pick one with a real random number
     (`shuf -i 1-12 -n 1`, or Python's `secrets`). Label it "as recalled".
  3. With neither, use any real chance source (the clock's seconds) and say
     which. Never fake randomness.

  Quote the one card you drew, apply it seriously, and note it in the
  work's file.
- **A random unrelated field.** Pick a field by real random draw, take how
  something works there, and apply it here.
- **Invert.** What's the opposite of the expected answer, and is any of it
  right?
- **Differ in kind, not styling.** Two concepts that differ only in colour
  or type are one concept.

Unexpected isn't enough on its own: the idea has to serve the intent and
the user's jobs. Never mark down a conventional answer that's genuinely
the best one.

## Writing

- **Point first.** The first line says the thing.
- **A concept, to the owner, is four lines:** a title, then one line each
  for the idea, why it fits the intent, and what it gives up.
- **Every update uses the same layout**, so the owner can scan it.
- Plain words. No jargon, no codes; name things by what they are.
- Longer reasoning goes in the work's files, not in your reply.
- Keep your reply to the orchestrator short: what you did, what's open,
  paths to anything longer.
